# Direct a code-rendered film
Adapted from Lemomo/LemoLab Lemo-Opuscar DIRECTOR.md; MIT/CC BY 4.0, see ATTRIBUTION.md.
1. Follow BRIEFING.md: ask one short question at a time for missing topic/learning objective, audience, key content, duration/platform, look, language and sound. Skip details already answered, and accept delegated preferences. Turn the answers into a reusable production prompt before the storyboard. Default to a clear 30–60 second lesson preview at 1280x720, 24 fps; use the production profile for 1080p delivery. Select semantic templates from EDUCATION.md. Offline supports captions or an uploaded recording only.
2. Choose one subject, one goal and one change. Draft three possible structures privately, then show the strongest as a scene table: time, framing, action, narration, music cue. Hook within three seconds and echo the opening in the ending.
3. Use the style's palette, material, motion and sound rules. Invent the subject and composition; do not reuse upstream demo plots, characters or melodies.
4. Use large readable shapes, safe caption margins and short sentences. Hold text long enough to read. Use camera moves for meaning; reserve a medium-native reveal for the turning point.
5. Show a storyboard and explicitly ask for confirmation. Render only after confirmation. On revision, revise the table and reconfirm before rendering.
6. Validate scene durations, narration length and music cue times. Preview real frames and inspect caption overlap, clipping, contrast and blank frames. Explain simplifications instead of pretending to reproduce a full upstream engine.
7. Deliver MP4, script, SRT and attribution. Distinguish tested capabilities from missing ones. Facts must come from user-provided evidence offline; never invent sources.
