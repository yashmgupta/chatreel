Essence, and what it is not:
1. 1. **One package system, used consistently.** Every graphic is built from the same few parts: a brand plate (parallelogram), a tab in the accent colour, a paper strap, ink cards with an accent top edge, mono datelines, stamps. Viewers read the system before…
2. 2. **The bulletin's order.** Sting → rundown ("today we have four stories") → item → item → … → back to the rundown, ticked off → sign-off. Each item opens with its category on the strap's tab and its date on the first

Editing grammar:
1. **120 BPM grid**, beat 0 at the end of the sting (1.5 s). Every cut and wipe on a beat or
2. **Shot lengths**: sting 1.5 s; rundown 4–5 s; story shots 3–7 s; a map long take (6–12 s) as the bulletin's breath; a comparison stays until every item has been fully on screen ≥ 2.5

Native moves:
1. 1. **Sting becomes the bug** — the brand plate slams in, then flies to the corner and stays there for the whole bulletin; opening and programme are one
2. 2. **Strap tab = category** — the strap's tab changes category (模型 MODELS, 市场 MARKETS, 监管 POLICY …) with a white flash when a new story starts; no separate item cards between

Material:
1. 1. **Facts first.** Every on-screen number and every sentence of the voice comes from an official source (the company's own page, the exchange's data, the institution's press release or speech), recorded in the implementation with claim, URL, quoted wording…
2. 2. **Data.** Draw charts from real series only; when you have just a few reported numbers, draw a *schematic* line, tag it the implementation, and label only what you know. Exchange closes: the exchange itself, the central bank's statistics portal, or a…
